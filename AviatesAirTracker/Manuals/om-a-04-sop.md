## Philosophy

Fly the aircraft first: **aviate, navigate, communicate**. Every AviatesAir flight is flown to the same standard whether it is recorded, online or offline, so that good habits become automatic.

## PF and PM duties

| Pilot Flying (PF) | Pilot Monitoring (PM) |
|---|---|
| Flight path and speed control | Radio communication |
| Aircraft configuration (calls for flap, gear) | Selects flap/gear on the PF's call and confirms |
| Navigation and automation mode selection | Monitors the flight path, calls deviations |
| Calls for checklists | Reads checklists |

Flying single-pilot in the simulator, you do both. Keep the discipline anyway: make the calls, then verify them.

## Flows and checklists

1. **Flows** configure the aircraft from memory, panel by panel.
2. **Checklists** then *verify* that the critical items were done. They are **read–verify**, not read–do.
3. Read the challenge, check the item, state the response. Stop at the **line** on Airbus checklists until the clearance is received.
4. Use the checklist for **your type**. The EFB **Checklists** app selects it from your booking automatically; the QRH has the same checklists in print.

## Briefings

**Departure briefing** (before start): runway and intersection, takeoff performance (flap, thrust, V-speeds), the SID and initial altitude, transition altitude, engine-failure and emergency turn procedure, return plan, taxi route and hot spots, weather and anything unusual.

**Approach briefing** (before top of descent): the ATIS, STAR and transition, approach type and minima, landing flap and autobrake, landing distance and runway exit, missed approach and go-around altitude, diversion fuel and the alternate, taxi to stand.

> **NOTE** A briefing is a plan you can fly, not a recital. Brief the threats first ("*what could catch us out today?*"), then the plan.

## Sterile flight deck

Below **10,000 ft**, and whenever on the ground with engines running, only operationally relevant activity takes place: no cabin PAs except safety-related ones, no chat, no photos, no reading the news. On a stream or voice channel, go quiet.

## Automation

- Use the **appropriate level** of automation. Hand-flying practice is encouraged in good weather and light traffic; **engage the autopilot** in busy airspace, in IMC, in RVSM airspace and when workload is high.
- **Know what the FMA says.** Read every mode change aloud.
- If the automation does something unexpected, **take it one level down** (managed → selected → manual) before troubleshooting.

## Altimeter setting

- **Departure:** set QNH before start. Set **STD (1013 / 29.92)** when passing the **transition altitude** in the climb.
- **Arrival:** set destination QNH when cleared to an altitude, or at the **transition level** in the descent.
- **Cross-check** both altimeters and the standby after every change. The EFB shows the planned transition altitude in Pilot Brief and Departure Perf.

## Speed limits

| Condition | Maximum |
|---|---|
| Below **FL100** (unless ATC or a published procedure says otherwise) | **250 kt IAS** |
| Holding (ICAO, up to FL140) | **230 kt** |
| Flap and gear extension | Placard speeds, see OM-B |
| Taxi, straight line | **30 kt** GS (20 kt on wet or contaminated surfaces) |
| Taxi, turns more than 30° and ramp areas | **10 kt** GS |

## Ground operations

- **Pushback:** only with the parking brake released on the tug's instruction, beacon **ON**, doors closed.
- **Engine start:** after pushback clearance. Heavies start the second engine(s) after the tug is disconnected, if the ground crew permit.
- **Taxi:** follow the cleared route with the airport chart displayed. Stop and ask when in doubt. Use taxi lights at night and runway turnoff lights as needed.
- **Single-engine taxi** is permitted for fuel saving where the add-on supports it: after landing when the engine has cooled (typically three minutes), and never on contaminated surfaces.
- **APU:** use ground power where available. Start the APU no earlier than necessary before pushback, and shut it down after arrival once ground power is connected.

## Noise abatement departure

Unless the airport or ATC specifies otherwise, fly **NADP 2**:

1. Takeoff thrust and V2 + 10–20 kt to **1,000 ft AAL**.
2. At 1,000 ft AAL: lower the nose, reduce to climb thrust, accelerate and retract flaps on schedule.
3. Climb at clean speed to 3,000 ft AAL, then accelerate to 250 kt.

Where the SID requires **NADP 1**, reduce to climb thrust at 800–1,500 ft but keep takeoff flap and V2 + 10–20 kt until **3,000 ft AAL**.
