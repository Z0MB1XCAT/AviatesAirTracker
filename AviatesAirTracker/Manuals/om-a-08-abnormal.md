## Handling a non-normal

1. **Fly the aircraft.** Maintain control and a safe flight path, then use the automation.
2. **Identify** the problem: ECAM/EICAS messages, warnings and indications.
3. Carry out the **immediate actions** (memory items) where they apply. See the [QRH](doc:qrh-00).
4. Work through the **ECAM or QRH checklist** for the type in your add-on.
5. **Decide:** continue, return or divert. Use a structured model such as **T-DODAR** (Time, Diagnose, Options, Decide, Assign, Review).
6. **Communicate** with ATC, and brief the cabin in the simulator.

## Declaring an emergency

| Call | Use for |
|---|---|
| **MAYDAY MAYDAY MAYDAY** | Grave and imminent danger requiring immediate assistance: engine fire, loss of all engines, depressurisation, smoke, MAYDAY FUEL. |
| **PAN PAN, PAN PAN, PAN PAN** | Urgency, where the safety of the aircraft or a person is at risk: engine failure after it's secured, medical emergency, a significant system failure. |

## Squawk codes

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **7700** | Emergency, unless ATC has already assigned another code. |
| **7600** | Radio failure. Follow the published loss-of-communications procedure. |
| **7500** | Unlawful interference. **Never used** at AviatesAir (see [OM-A 7](doc:om-a-07#non-normals-online)). |

## Diversion

Divert when continuing to destination is no longer safe or sensible:

- weather below minima with no improvement expected within your fuel,
- a non-normal requiring a **landing at the nearest suitable airport** (fire not confirmed out, smoke, a single remaining power source, the ECAM/QRH says so),
- a medical emergency,
- fuel below what is needed for destination plus reserves.

A diverted flight is a normal outcome. Land, park, and let the Tracker complete the flight. Explain the diversion in the PIREP comment.

## Overweight landing

Landing above MLW is permitted **only** when a delay to reduce weight would compromise safety (fire, smoke, medical). Fly a stabilised approach at the correct VAPP with the maximum landing flap, and land smoothly: aim for **less than 360 fpm** at touchdown. An overweight landing always requires an inspection, so report it.

## Rejected takeoff

- **Below 80 kt** (100 kt on the Airbus fleet): reject for any significant failure or warning.
- **80 kt / 100 kt to V1:** reject only for engine failure or fire, a predictive windshear warning, unsafe configuration warnings or if the aircraft is unsafe or unable to fly.
- **After V1:** continue the takeoff. The problem is dealt with in the air.

The PF calls "**STOP**" and performs the RTO actions (see [QRH](doc:qrh-00#rejected-takeoff)).

## Tail strike and hard landing

- **Suspected tail strike:** don't pressurise the aircraft further than needed. Return to the departure airport for inspection (see [CN-002](doc:cn-002)).
- **Hard landing** above 800 fpm, or a heavy bounce: the aircraft needs an inspection before the next flight. The PIREP is held for review automatically; add your comment.

## Reporting occurrences

Report in your PIREP comment (or to staff on Discord) any of: a tail strike, a hard or overweight landing, a landing below final reserve fuel, a runway excursion, a TCAS RA, a GPWS warning, a windshear encounter, a level bust, or any event you think others could learn from. Reports are handled under our **just culture** policy ([OM-A 6](doc:om-a-06#just-culture)).
