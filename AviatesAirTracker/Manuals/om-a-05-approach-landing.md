## Stabilised approach criteria

An approach is **stabilised** when **all** of the following are true:

1. The aircraft is on the correct **lateral and vertical flight path**, needing only small changes of heading and pitch.
2. It is in the **landing configuration**: gear down, landing flap set (at or above the company minimum for the type; see [CN-003](doc:cn-003)).
3. The speed is between **VREF (VLS on Airbus) and VAPP + 10 kt**, and not decreasing below the target.
4. The **rate of descent is no greater than 1,000 fpm**. If a higher rate is expected (a steep approach or a high groundspeed), it must be briefed.
5. **Thrust** is stabilised, spooled up and appropriate for the configuration.
6. **Bank angle** is no more than 10° below 500 ft; wings level by 50 ft except for crosswind correction.
7. All **briefings and checklists** are complete. The LANDING checklist is done by 1,000 ft.

### Gates

| Gate | Height | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **IMC gate** | **1,000 ft AAL** | Stabilised, in IMC and on every instrument approach. |
| **VMC gate** | **500 ft AAL** | Stabilised, in visual conditions on a visual approach. Configuration must be complete by 1,000 ft. |

> **WARNING** If the approach is **not stabilised at the gate**, or becomes unstable below it, a **GO-AROUND is mandatory**. There is no "just this once".

The EFB Landing report shows your 1,000 ft and 500 ft gate states from the recorded approach, and the Tracker holds a PIREP for review when gear, flap or speed were not stabilised by 1,000 ft (see [OM-A 6](doc:om-a-06)).

## Go-around policy

AviatesAir has a **no-fault go-around policy**. Nobody is ever criticised for going around. A go-around is a normal manoeuvre, and a well-flown one is a sign of good airmanship. Go around when:

- the approach is not stabilised at the gate,
- the required visual reference is not established at the minima,
- there is a runway incursion, an obstacle, or an ATC instruction to go around,
- windshear, a long flare or a bounce means you can't touch down safely in the touchdown zone,
- you have any doubt.

After a go-around, fly the published missed approach unless ATC instructs otherwise. Re-brief before a second attempt. After **two** unsuccessful approaches, divert unless the reason has clearly gone away.

## Landing technique

- **Aim** for the touchdown aiming point (the large markings about 1,000 ft / 300–400 m past the threshold). Cross the threshold at about **50 ft**.
- **Touchdown zone:** touch down within the **touchdown zone**, the first **3,000 ft (900 m)** past the threshold (or the first third of a shorter runway), ideally close to the aiming point. A touchdown beyond it is a **long landing**, and one less than 200 ft past the threshold is a **short landing**. If the touchdown point is running long, go around rather than chase it.
- **Flare** at the type's normal height (roughly 20–30 ft on narrow-bodies, higher on heavies), reduce thrust smoothly and **don't float**. A firm, on-speed touchdown in the zone beats a long "butter".
- **Crosswind:** use the type's recommended technique. Crab to the flare, then de-crab with rudder, cross-controlling with aileron as needed so the aircraft touches down aligned with the centreline.
- Lower the nose wheel promptly and smoothly. **Stay on the centreline.**

## Deceleration

- **Autobrake:** select to match the runway: LOW/2 for long dry runways, MED/3 for normal or wet runways, MAX only for short or contaminated runways (BTV on the A350/A380 where available).
- **Reverse thrust:** idle reverse at least on every landing where available. Full reverse on short, wet or contaminated runways, cancelled by about 60 kt (per type).
- Plan the **runway exit** in the approach briefing and aim to vacate without delay, especially at busy online events.

## Landing flap policy

Use the **standard landing flap** for your type unless there is a reason not to. The **company minimum** in [CN-003](doc:cn-003) applies to the 737 and A320 family.
