Real airlines run a **Flight Data Monitoring (FDM)** programme: every flight's data is recorded and analysed to find trends and improve safety. AviatesAir does the same with the Tracker. The aim is **learning, not punishment**.

## What is recorded

For every flight: the flight phases, route and altitude profile, block and air times, fuel, and every landing. For every landing: the full approach from about 2½ minutes before touchdown through the rollout (altitude, speeds, vertical speed, pitch, bank, flap and gear), plus the touchdown values. You can review any of your last 25 landings in the EFB **Landing** app.

## Landing score

Each landing is scored out of **100** on six factors:

| Factor | Points | Full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical speed at touchdown | **30** | ≤ 250 fpm (25 pts to 400, 15 to 600, 8 to 800, 3 to 1,000) |
| Pitch attitude at touchdown | **20** | 1.5–3.5° nose-up (15 pts 0.5–5°, 8 pts 0–7°) |
| Bank angle at touchdown | **15** | < 1° (12 < 2°, 8 < 3°, 4 < 5°) |
| Speed at touchdown | **15** | 100–145 kt IAS (10 pts 80–160 kt, 5 pts 60–180 kt) |
| Crosswind component | **10** | < 2 kt, scaling down to 1 pt at 15 kt or more |
| Approach stability | **10** | Stabilised approach (2 pts if not) |

> **NOTE** The speed band suits the narrow-body fleet. Heavies (A380, 747-8, 777F) naturally touch down faster and lose a few points there. Compare your heavy landings with each other, not with the A320.

| Score | Grade |
|---|---|
| 90–100 | **EXCELLENT** |
| 75–89 | **GOOD** |
| 60–74 | **FAIR** |
| 40–59 | **HARD** |
| 0–39 | **VERY HARD** |

The **butter rating** (PURE BUTTER · BUTTER · SMOOTH · SOLID · FIRM · HARD) is the fun part: it's based on vertical speed alone and has no bearing on review.

> **NOTE** A *SOLID* 200–300 fpm touchdown in the touchdown zone is exactly what airlines want, especially on a wet or short runway. Don't float the aircraft down the runway chasing a butter landing.

## FDM events

The landing report lists exceedances as **WARNING** (red), **CAUTION** (amber) or **NOTE**. These are AviatesAir's event limits, not aircraft limitations:

| Event | Caution | Warning |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical speed at touchdown | ≥ 600 fpm | ≥ 800 fpm |
| Pitch at touchdown (tail-strike margin) | ≥ 7.5° (or nose-low < 0°) | ≥ 9.0° |
| Bank at touchdown | ≥ 3° | ≥ 5° |
| Bounce | 1 bounce | 2 or more |
| Touchdown past the threshold | > 2,500 ft | > 3,000 ft (long landing) |
| Touchdown short | n/a | < 200 ft past the threshold |
| Centreline deviation | ≥ 30 ft | ≥ 60 ft |
| Rate of descent below 1,000 ft AAL | ≥ 1,000 fpm | ≥ 1,500 fpm |
| Bank below 500 ft | ≥ 10° | ≥ 15° |
| Unstabilised at a gate | 1,000 ft gate | 500 ft gate |
| Tailwind at touchdown | n/a | ≥ 10 kt |
| Crosswind component | ≥ 25 kt | n/a |
| Crab angle at touchdown | ≥ 5° | n/a |
| No flare detected | note | n/a |

## Held for review

A PIREP is **held for review** by staff, not rejected, when any of the following occurred:

- **Hard landing:** touchdown vertical speed above **800 fpm**,
- a **bounced** landing,
- **gear, landing flap or approach speed not stabilised by 1,000 ft AGL** (landing flap at or above the company minimum counts; see [CN-003](doc:cn-003)),
- touchdown more than **60 ft** from the centreline, or outside **200–3,000 ft** past the threshold (where the runway is in the Tracker's database),
- a climb or descent rate above **8,000 fpm** at any point,
- cruise airspeed outside a safe band (jets 250–530 kt IAS, turboprops 150–300 kt),
- off blocks more than **20 minutes** after the scheduled departure.

When a flight is held, the Tracker asks for your comment. **Explain what happened** (for example "gusting 25 kt, bounced and went around", or "ATC kept us high, descent rate 1,400 fpm to regain the path"). Honest context is all that's needed.

## Just culture

- Honest mistakes and honest reports are **never** punished. We learn from them.
- **Recklessness** is not acceptable: deliberate, repeated disregard of SOPs, limits or ATC, or manipulating the simulator to fake a flight.
- Trends matter more than single events. Staff may offer tips or a check flight if the same event keeps recurring.
